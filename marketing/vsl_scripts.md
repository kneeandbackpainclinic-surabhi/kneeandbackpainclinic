# Dr. Surabhi Vaidya: Test-Led Landing Page VSL

**Objective:** Encourage the visitor to complete the short test and receive a suggested next path.
**Recommended length:** 5 to 7 minutes
**Single CTA:** `Take the test`
**Test outcome:** A suggested next step of either joining the educational webinar or booking a personal consultation.

## Scene 1: Begin with the person's everyday story

**Visuals:** A group of senior adults spending time in a garden. One person places a hand on the knee and rises carefully from a chair. Continue with natural scenes of walking, stairs, travel and family time.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Knee or back discomfort often becomes noticeable through small changes in everyday life.
>
> Standing after sitting may take a little longer. Stairs may require more thought. A morning walk may become shorter. Travel, prayer, work or spending time with family may begin to feel more difficult.
>
> These changes can affect much more than comfort. They can affect confidence, independence and the freedom to plan the day without constantly thinking about movement.
>
> If this feels familiar, the next useful step is not to assume that one treatment will suit every person. It is to understand your situation and identify which type of conversation may be most useful for you right now.”

**On-screen text:** `A clearer next step begins with your story`

## Scene 2: Meet Dr. Surabhi

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi entering the clinic, speaking with a patient, reviewing notes and demonstrating a movement assessment. Display only verified credentials.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “I am Dr. Surabhi Vaidya, an Ayurvedic physician with a BAMS and an MBA in Clinical Research. At Charak Health Solutions in Thane, I work with people seeking help for knee pain, back pain, stiffness, sciatica and difficulty with everyday movement.
>
> Many people reach the clinic after trying several approaches. Some have used medicines repeatedly. Some have tried exercise, physiotherapy, injections or other therapies. Others have been advised to consider surgery and want to understand whether any appropriate non-surgical options should be discussed first.
>
> My role is to understand the person, assess the condition carefully and explain realistic options. If another specialist or surgical opinion is required, that should also be discussed honestly.”

**On-screen text:** `Dr. Surabhi Vaidya | BAMS | MBA Clinical Research | Thane`

## Scene 3: Why the same advice does not suit everyone

**Visuals:** Two people with similar discomfort but different routines, movement patterns and goals.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Pain is a symptom. Stiffness is a symptom. Difficulty climbing stairs is a symptom. These experiences matter, but they do not provide the complete diagnosis by themselves.
>
> Two people may describe similar discomfort and still have different health histories, movement patterns, activity levels, joint conditions and personal goals.
>
> One person may be at an early stage and mainly want reliable information. Another may have persistent symptoms, significant difficulty with daily activities or an earlier recommendation for surgery. That person may benefit from a direct clinical conversation sooner.
>
> This is why the landing page includes a short test. It helps us understand where you are in the decision process before suggesting a next path.”

**On-screen text:** `Different situations need different next steps`

## Scene 4: What the test asks

**Visuals:** Show the test interface on a mobile phone. Animate one question at a time. Do not display a diagnosis or medical score.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “The test takes about one minute and asks a few simple questions.
>
> It asks how long the discomfort has been present, how much it is affecting everyday activities, what approaches have already been tried, whether surgery has been discussed and what you are hoping to do next.
>
> There are no complicated medical terms, and you do not need to upload reports. Your answers are used only to suggest which next conversation may be more appropriate.
>
> The test is not a diagnosis. It cannot confirm the cause of pain or decide which treatment is suitable. That requires an individual clinical assessment.”

**On-screen text:** `About 1 minute | Simple questions | No online diagnosis`

## Scene 5: Suggested path one, the live Hindi webinar

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi teaching online, a simple webinar slide and a family watching together.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “If your answers show that you are still exploring, want to understand the subject better or are not ready for an in-clinic assessment, the suggested path may be my live Hindi webinar.
>
> The webinar explains common questions about knee and back discomfort, why assessment matters, how movement and lifestyle may fit into care, and how different treatment choices are considered.
>
> We also discuss the surgery question honestly. Some people may have non-surgical options to explore, while others may require specialist or surgical care. No responsible answer can be given without understanding the individual case.
>
> The webinar is designed to help you and your family ask better questions before making a treatment decision.”

**On-screen text:** `Suggested path: Learn first through the live Hindi webinar`

## Scene 6: Suggested path two, personal consultation

**Visuals:** In-clinic history discussion, report review, posture observation, movement assessment and Naadi Pariksha.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “If your answers suggest that discomfort is significantly affecting daily movement, several treatments have already been tried, surgery has been advised or you are ready for individual guidance, the suggested path may be a personal consultation.
>
> The consultation begins with a detailed health and symptom history. We discuss earlier treatment, relevant reports, current medicines and the activities that have become difficult.
>
> I observe posture, alignment and relevant movement. I also use Naadi Pariksha as part of my Ayurvedic assessment. This helps me determine whether care at our clinic may be appropriate and what expectations are realistic.
>
> If treatment at Charak Health Solutions is suitable, the plan may include selected Ayurvedic therapies, Marma Chikitsa, movement guidance, diet, posture advice and follow-up. Not every person requires every therapy, and results can vary.”

**On-screen text:** `Suggested path: Discuss your situation in a personal consultation`

## Scene 7: Why the suggested path matters

**Visuals:** A simple forked journey graphic beginning with the test and leading to webinar or consultation.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “The purpose of the test is not to label your condition. It is to reduce confusion about what to do next.
>
> Someone who is still learning may not need to book a consultation immediately. The webinar can provide a useful foundation.
>
> Someone whose movement is significantly limited, whose symptoms have persisted or who has already tried several approaches may need a more direct conversation.
>
> In both cases, the aim is the same: to move from general information toward a next step that fits the person's current situation.”

**On-screen text:** `One short test | One suggested next path`

## Scene 8: Final invitation

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi speaking directly to camera. Show the test on a mobile phone and finish with one clear button.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “If knee or back discomfort is raising questions about movement, independence or treatment choices, begin with the short test below.
>
> Answer the questions based on your current experience. At the end, you will receive one suggested next path: either join the live Hindi webinar to understand your options, or book a personal consultation for an individual assessment.
>
> The result is only a guide and not a diagnosis. You remain in control of the decision.
>
> Take the test now and find the next suggested path for you.”

**Final on-screen CTA:** `Take the test`

## Production notes

- Record the master in horizontal 16:9 for the landing page.
- Capture selected B-roll vertically for Reels and Shorts.
- Use large subtitles with no more than two lines at a time.
- Show the actual mobile test interface during Scene 4 and Scene 8.
- Use the real clinic, Dr. Surabhi and the clinic team where possible.
- Obtain written consent before showing any identifiable patient or treatment footage.
- Do not use fixed relief percentages, guaranteed outcomes, cartilage-regrowth promises or universal surgery-avoidance claims.
- Keep only one clickable CTA next to the VSL: `Take the test`.
