# Dr. Surabhi Vaidya: Landing Page VSL Scripts

Two dedicated versions are provided. Each version ends with one CTA only.

## Version 1: Webinar VSL

**Objective:** Help people who are still exploring understand the value of the live Hindi webinar.  
**Recommended length:** 5 to 7 minutes  
**CTA:** `Join webinar`

### Scene 1: Why knee and back pain decisions feel confusing

**Visuals:** Senior adults in a garden, one person rising carefully from a chair, followed by calm family and walking scenes.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “When knee or back discomfort begins affecting daily life, people often receive many different suggestions. One person recommends rest. Another recommends exercise. Someone may suggest medicines, injections, therapy or surgery. Online videos may promise a quick cure, while family members may share completely different experiences.
>
> With so much information, the first challenge is often not finding another treatment. It is understanding which questions should be asked before choosing one.
>
> I am Dr. Surabhi Vaidya from Charak Health Solutions in Thane. I have created a free live Hindi webinar to help people and their families understand knee and back pain in a clear, practical and responsible way.”

**On-screen text:** `Clear information before treatment decisions`

### Scene 2: What symptoms can and cannot tell us

**Visuals:** Simple diagram of the knee and spine, plus examples of stairs, standing from a chair and walking.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Pain is a symptom. Stiffness is a symptom. Difficulty climbing stairs is a symptom. These experiences matter, but they do not provide the complete diagnosis by themselves.
>
> Two people can describe similar discomfort and still have different health histories, movement patterns, levels of joint damage and personal goals. This is why a treatment that helped one person may not be appropriate for another.
>
> In the webinar, I will explain how clinicians think about symptoms, daily function, posture, movement and previous treatment. I will also explain why an individual assessment becomes important when symptoms persist or begin limiting independence.”

**On-screen text:** `Similar symptoms can require different advice`

### Scene 3: The everyday signs worth discussing

**Visuals:** Stairs, morning movement, travel, prayer, walking and getting into a car.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Many people wait until discomfort becomes severe. Earlier changes may be easier to overlook. Standing after sitting may take longer. Morning stiffness may last longer than before. A person may begin avoiding stairs, long journeys, floor sitting or evening walks.
>
> These changes do not automatically mean that surgery is required. They also should not be dismissed as a normal part of age without proper evaluation.
>
> During the live session, we will discuss the signs that deserve attention, the information to note before meeting a clinician and the situations that require prompt medical evaluation.”

**On-screen text:** `Notice changes in function, not pain alone`

### Scene 4: Understanding treatment choices

**Visuals:** Neutral icons for exercise, medicines, therapies, consultation and surgery. Avoid frightening procedure imagery.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “People frequently ask whether exercise is safe, whether medicines only mask pain, whether Ayurveda can help and whether an advised operation can be delayed.
>
> These questions do not have one universal answer. Appropriately selected movement and strengthening can support many people, but the right starting point depends on the condition. Medicines, rehabilitation, Ayurvedic care and surgical opinions each have a place in healthcare when they are used for the right person and the right reason.
>
> My goal in the webinar is to help you understand how these choices are considered, what realistic expectations look like and why guaranteed claims should be treated carefully.”

**On-screen text:** `Right option. Right person. Realistic expectation.`

### Scene 5: Dr. Surabhi's assessment-led approach

**Visuals:** Clinic footage showing a history discussion, posture observation, movement review and Naadi Pariksha.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “At Charak Health Solutions, my consultation begins with the person rather than a fixed package. I review the health history, current symptoms, earlier treatment, reports, medicines and the daily activities that have become difficult.
>
> I observe posture and relevant movement and use Naadi Pariksha as part of my Ayurvedic assessment. If care at the clinic is suitable, an individual plan may include selected Ayurvedic therapies, Marma Chikitsa, movement guidance, diet, posture advice and follow-up.
>
> Not every person needs every therapy. Results and treatment duration can differ, which is why assessment and monitoring matter.”

**On-screen text:** `History | Movement | Assessment | Individual plan`

### Scene 6: The surgery question

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi speaking directly to camera with a simple decision path.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Can surgery always be avoided? No responsible answer can be given without understanding the individual case.
>
> Some people may have appropriate non-surgical options to explore. Some may improve function or delay a procedure. Others may have advanced damage or warning signs that require an orthopaedic or specialist opinion.
>
> In the webinar, I will explain how to approach this question without fear and without false promises. The purpose is to help families make informed decisions and know when a personal assessment is the right next step.”

**On-screen text:** `Honest information. No universal promises.`

### Scene 7: Invitation

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi speaking warmly to camera. Show the webinar title, date and time.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “If you are still comparing options, supporting a family member or trying to understand what questions to ask, this live Hindi webinar is a useful place to begin.
>
> You will learn how knee and back pain are assessed, what everyday signs matter, how treatment choices are considered and when individual advice is necessary. The session is educational and does not replace a diagnosis or personal consultation.
>
> Join the webinar using the button below. Enter your name and WhatsApp number, and the clinic will send the confirmation and joining details directly on WhatsApp. I look forward to meeting you in the live session.”

**Final on-screen CTA:** `Join webinar`

## Version 2: Consultation VSL

**Objective:** Help higher-intent visitors understand the consultation and book an in-clinic assessment.  
**Recommended length:** 6 to 8 minutes  
**CTA:** `Book appointment`

### Scene 1: When daily movement starts changing

**Visuals:** A senior adult standing from a garden chair, using stairs carefully, entering a car and walking with family.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Knee and back discomfort often becomes noticeable through small changes in everyday life. Standing after sitting takes longer. Stairs require more thought. A morning walk becomes shorter. Travel, prayer, work and family activities may begin to feel more difficult.
>
> These changes can affect confidence and independence as much as they affect comfort. When symptoms persist or several treatments have already been tried, the next useful step is a proper individual assessment.
>
> I am Dr. Surabhi Vaidya from Charak Health Solutions in Thane. My consultation process is designed to understand the person, assess the condition and explain suitable options with realistic expectations.”

**On-screen text:** `A structured assessment for a clearer next step`

### Scene 2: Who commonly comes to the clinic

**Visuals:** Clinic arrival, consultation desk and a family member accompanying a patient.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Many people visit after months or years of recurring discomfort. Some have used pain medicines repeatedly. Some have tried exercise, physiotherapy, injections or different therapies. Others have been advised to consider knee replacement or spine surgery and want to understand whether appropriate non-surgical options should be explored.
>
> A consultation is especially useful when daily function is being affected, symptoms keep returning, progress has stopped or the available advice feels confusing.
>
> The aim is not to dismiss earlier treatment. It is to review the complete picture and decide what may be appropriate now.”

**On-screen text:** `Persistent symptoms | Limited movement | Unclear next step`

### Scene 3: Why the same treatment does not suit everyone

**Visuals:** Two different people with similar discomfort but different routines and movement patterns.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “Knee pain is a symptom, not a complete diagnosis. Back pain is also a symptom. Two people may both experience discomfort on the stairs, but their medical history, posture, muscle strength, activity level, joint condition and personal goals may be different.
>
> One person may want to return to a daily walk. Another may want to travel comfortably, sit for prayer or manage stairs independently.
>
> This is why I do not begin with a standard treatment package. I begin by understanding what is happening, what has already been tried and what improvement would be meaningful for that individual.”

**On-screen text:** `The plan begins with the person`

### Scene 4: What happens during the consultation

**Visuals:** History discussion, report review, posture observation, movement assessment and Naadi Pariksha.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “The consultation begins with a detailed health and pain history. We discuss when the symptoms began, what makes them better or worse, earlier injuries, medical conditions, current medicines and treatments already tried.
>
> We then discuss daily function. This may include walking, standing from a chair, climbing stairs, bending, sleeping, driving or travelling. I observe posture, alignment and relevant movements. I also use Naadi Pariksha as part of my Ayurvedic assessment.
>
> Please bring relevant reports and a list of current medicines if available. The purpose is to make the discussion specific, reduce guesswork and determine whether care at our clinic may be suitable.”

**On-screen text:** `45-minute in-clinic assessment | Thane`

### Scene 5: How the care plan may be built

**Visuals:** Calm clips of Marma Chikitsa, Pottali Sekam, medicated steam, Lepam, guided movement and diet discussion.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “When treatment at Charak Health Solutions is suitable, the plan may combine several elements. These can include Marma Chikitsa, selected Ayurvedic therapies such as Pottali Sekam, medicated steam or Lepam, and guidance on movement, diet, posture and home practices.
>
> These techniques are selected according to the assessment. Not everyone requires every therapy, and treatment duration can vary.
>
> Progress should be connected to practical goals, such as greater walking tolerance, less stiffness, more confidence on stairs or standing more comfortably. The possible result depends on the individual condition, its stage, overall health and response to care.”

**On-screen text:** `Selected therapies | Practical goals | Monitored progress`

### Scene 6: An honest approach to surgery

**Visuals:** Direct-to-camera explanation with a simple, neutral decision graphic.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “One of the most common questions is whether surgery can be avoided. That decision cannot be made through a website, advertisement or questionnaire.
>
> Some people may have non-surgical options that are appropriate to explore. Others may have advanced damage, urgent warning signs or a condition that requires orthopaedic or specialist care.
>
> My responsibility is to explain the assessment honestly. Surgery should not be used to frighten people, and non-surgical treatment should not be presented as a guarantee. If another opinion or referral is needed, that should be discussed clearly.”

**On-screen text:** `Assessment before decisions | Referral when required`

### Scene 7: Trust and patient experience

**Visuals:** Approved patient clips, the clinic team and the clinic's verified Google review page.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “On this page, you can hear from patients who have visited the clinic and read reviews shared on Google. These experiences can show what the care journey felt like for those individuals.
>
> A testimonial cannot predict another person's result. Each condition and response can differ. What every patient should expect is an opportunity to be heard, a structured assessment and an understandable explanation of the available next steps.”

**On-screen text:** `Real experiences | Individual results vary`

### Scene 8: Book the consultation

**Visuals:** Dr. Surabhi in the consultation room, followed by the clinic address and appointment button.

**Dr. Surabhi:**

> “If knee or back discomfort is affecting everyday movement, several treatments have already been tried, or a personal assessment is now needed, I invite you to book an appointment at Charak Health Solutions in Thane.
>
> The consultation is a focused opportunity to review the history, movement, reports and realistic options for the individual situation.
>
> Use the button below to book an appointment. Select an available time and the clinic will share the confirmation details. I look forward to meeting you and helping you understand the next step clearly.”

**Final on-screen CTA:** `Book appointment`

## Production notes for both VSLs

- Record the landing-page masters in horizontal 16:9.
- Capture selected B-roll vertically so scenes can also become Reels and Shorts.
- Keep subtitles visible and limit each subtitle block to two lines.
- Use the real clinic and team where possible.
- Obtain written consent for identifiable patient footage and testimonials.
- Display credentials, location and rating only when the exact facts are verified.
- Do not include fixed relief percentages, guaranteed outcomes, cartilage-regrowth promises or universal surgery-avoidance claims.
- Keep the webinar VSL CTA exclusively as Join webinar.
- Keep the consultation VSL CTA exclusively as Book appointment.
